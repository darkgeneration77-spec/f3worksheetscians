Section C — Structured Answer Review

1. Reproductive system

B = prostate gland. It secretes a milky white fluid that activates or increases sperm mobility.

Similarities of sperm and ovum: both are reproductive cells; production of both involves cell division.
Differences: male gametes are produced by testes while female gametes are produced by ovaries; sperm has a tail while ovum has no tail and is round.

Female secondary sexual characteristics stated: growth of acne; development of breasts and hips.

2. Immunity

White blood cells produce antibodies and ingest/digest using enzymes.
Immunity is the ability of the body system to fight pathogens before infection.
Types listed: naturally acquired active, artificially acquired active, naturally acquired passive, artificially acquired passive immunity.

3. Heating and cooling water

Stage duration: 10 minutes.
Water changes from liquid to gaseous state.
Volume during CD is less than during AB because some water turned to steam during BC.
300 ml takes longer than 150 ml for the same process because more heat energy is required.
For salt water: maximum temperature slightly above 100°C; minimum temperature slightly below 0°C.

4. Eye and vision

Ciliary muscle function: change the thickness of the eye lens.
For a near object: ciliary muscles contract; lens becomes more curved and thicker.
Eyeball too long: short-sightedness.
Correction stated: concave lens.
Another symptom listed in the answer file: cannot see near objects clearly but can see far objects clearly.

5. Cellular respiration and photosynthesis

Cell respiration uses oxygen and glucose to produce energy, carbon dioxide and water.
Oxygen + Glucose → Energy + Carbon dioxide + Water.
The answer distinguishes cellular respiration from breathing: respiration oxidises glucose to release energy, while breathing is the mechanical intake of oxygen through respiratory organs.
Similarities with photosynthesis: both require energy and involve gas exchange. Differences include sunlight dependence, reactants/products, and chloroplast vs mitochondria.

6. Radioactivity — Iodine-131

An unstable iodine atom undergoes radioactive decay to become a more stable xenon atom by emitting electrons as beta particles.
Half-life: time taken for the number of undecayed nuclei in a sample to reduce to half its original number.
Beta particles penetrate paper but not aluminium and lead; the answer describes beta as having moderate penetrating power.
