Section C

1. Electrolysis of water

1. Electrolysis: a process whereby a compound is decomposed into its constituent elements when an electric current passes through an electrolyte.
2. Water molecule: hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom.
3. K: water and oxygen gas; L: hydrogen gas.
4. Sulphuric acid is added to increase electrical conductivity of water.

3. Immunity

1. Vaccine contains dead or weakened pathogen used to induce immunity.
2. Second injection increases antibody concentration, raises it above immunity level and helps achieve permanent immunity.
3. Both active and passive immunity can be natural or artificial; both involve antibodies.
4. White blood cell. Any two: has nucleus, no pigment, no fixed shape, produced in bone marrow, larger than red blood cell.

4. Effects of cigarette smoke

1. Use a vacuum pump to pull cigarette smoke into the apparatus.
2. Cotton wool changes from white to brown; thermometer reading increases.
3. Cigarette smoke contains tar and is hot.
4. Bicarbonate indicator changes from red to yellow because cigarette smoke is acidic.
5. Cigarettes contain carcinogenic compounds that stimulate uncontrolled cell division, forming cancer cells which attack nearby cells.

5. Reflection of light

1. Light can be reflected.
2. Angle of incidence = angle of reflection; incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
3. Light rays must be parallel; reflecting surface must be flat, smooth and shiny.
4. Reflection occurs when light rays are directed to the surface of a plane mirror.
5. The back of the plane mirror is coated with silver nitrate or shiny aluminium.
6. New angle of reflection = 20° + 10° = 30°.

6. Reactivity series

3. Ascending order: X, Z, W, Y.
4. W = Aluminium; X = Copper; Y = Magnesium; Z = Carbon.
5. Z is carbon; it reacts with oxygen to form carbon dioxide gas, so no residue is produced.
6. No, because Z is more reactive than W.
