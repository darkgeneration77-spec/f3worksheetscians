Section C — Structured Questions

1. Three-pin plug

1. A = earth wire, green; B = neutral wire, blue; C = live wire, red.
2. Wire A connects to the metal part of the heater.
3. The plug casing is plastic, an insulator, and a fuse breaks the circuit when excessive current flows.
4. Earthing protects a person from electric shock by allowing leakage current to flow to the ground instead of through the person.

2. Vision and hearing

The animal has a wider field of vision, allowing it to detect predators and escape.
The bat uses stereoscopic hearing.
This allows the bat to detect prey as a food source and detect predators, helping it avoid danger.

3. Guttation

1. Guttation.
2. Guttation is secretion of water through hydathode pores, while transpiration is loss of water as water vapour through evaporation. Guttation occurs when transpiration cannot be carried out due to high humidity or watery soil.
3. It removes excess water from plants when root pressure is high and transpiration rate is low.
4. Any two: air movement, air humidity, light intensity, temperature.

4. Reactivity of metals with oxygen

Observations: Aluminium burns with a bright flame; Zinc burns fairly bright; Iron glows dimly; Lead glows dimly.
2. Magnesium produces the brightest flame.
3. Magnesium is the most reactive metal with oxygen.
4. Potassium manganate(VII): to supply oxygen.
5. Ascending reactivity: lead → iron → zinc → aluminium → magnesium.

5. Silicon compounds

1. Silica = silicon + oxygen; silicate = metal + silicon + oxygen.
2. Stable properties: do not dissolve in water, do not break down when heated, do not react with dilute acid.
3. Silica: sand, quartz. Silicate: clay, asbestos.
4. Sand is used to make cement and bricks; clay is used to make ceramic objects.

6. Surface water and underground water

1. Surface water forms when rainwater does not diffuse into the ground, or water that has diffused resurfaces through springs.
2. Example: natural lake.
3. Use: daily use.
4. Underground water is water found between soil and rocks beneath the ground. Most comes from rainwater that diffuses into soil; rocks or soil holding it are aquifers, and the water can return to the surface through springs.
